04 Timeline

The date that sets the pace

The treaties incepted 1 July 2026. The first quarter closes 30 September. Accounts are due 45 days later — 14 November 2026, which is a Saturday, so the statement must be issued by Friday 13 November.

That is 54 working days between 1 September and the due date, before public holidays. The plan below is 53 working days of work on one developer, and the statement is issued Thursday 12 November — one day before it is due.

The first window came in ahead of plan. On 27 August this called for ten days to 14 September to close the slip faults, wire the cession and populate Engineering. That work finished on 1 September, and the four days recovered have gone into the statement of account rather than into the margin at the end.

Contingency is carried inside each task, one day apiece, rather than held as a block at the end. A task that runs to its estimate absorbs its own slip and the following window still starts on time. A task that overruns by more than a day pushes every window after it, and there is no reserve at the end to recover from.

06 Recommendations

01
Get the routing signed off this week

It is a half day of work and it is the last structural piece of the cession. It changes what is ceded on every policy, so it needs a signature rather than a switch — and it is holding up the only part of the cession still running the old way.

02
Chase the signing schedule alongside it

About 34 points of cession are unnamed. It is the one item that stops a statement being issued at all, it is entirely outside our control, and every day it is outstanding comes off the delivery margin.

03
Build the statement of account to generate itself

The 45-day render, the 14-day confirmation and the reserve-deposit interest all repeat every quarter, for as long as the treaty runs. Build them as scheduled jobs, never as a manual spreadsheet step.
