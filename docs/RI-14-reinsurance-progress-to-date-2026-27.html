01 The short version

  • The 2026/27 treaty year is configured and live. Both treaties are in — General Quota Share and Motor Quota Share, 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027 — with their layers attached and the regulatory mapping seeded and written to the database.
  • The cession now reconciles to Reinsurance’s own workbook, to the cent. The test policy totals 687,704,212 against their 687,704,212, group by group. That took rebuilding the read: the figures come from the table the treaty actually cedes from, not from the coverage rows, which never carried motor at all.
  • Their own test cases found two faults in our engine, and we fixed both. Fourteen of their eighteen now reproduce exactly; the other four are the basis they themselves superseded on 24 August, and our figures follow the later one. All eighteen are re-performed on every build, so their Control C3 is discharged by running the suite rather than by remembering to.
  • The FAC register is built and handed over for testing. Every item Reinsurance reported has been closed, and so have the four faults we found ourselves.
  • The quarterly statement of account has not been started. That is the piece with a contractual deadline — 13 November 2026 — and it is the whole of the remaining risk. Everything else open is finishing work.

03 What is live, and what is still in our hands

First what has closed since the last report, then what the reconciliation proved, then the three items in our hands to finish. The items needing a decision or a document from the business are listed in RI-13.

Closed since the last report

All six items that were ours on 27 August are finished.

  • All three faults in the facultative slip are closed. Filing a signed slip now records the reinsurer’s commitment and closes the panel; deductible, description of risk and territorial scope can be typed in; and the slip prints the currency the placement is actually in, and refuses to add two currencies into one total.
  • The cession is wired to the regulatory mapping. The calculator now runs against real policies. It sits behind a flag that still defaults to the old routing, because switching it changes what is ceded — that sign-off is item 2 of the business list in RI-13.
  • Engineering is created and populated, and the Accident remap is written to the database — 56 coverage rows across five groups.
  • The 40,000,000 surplus is evidenced. The Final Terms show a retention of 10,000,000 and four lines. Reinsurance’s own work paper doubted this; the doubt assumed the lines sat on the 3,000,000 net retention leg, and they do not. No question needed to be asked.
  • The catastrophe programme has been received and its limits are loaded — 70,000,000 per event on both General and Motor, 120,000,000 on the surplus, and 50,000,000 on motor riot and strike.

What the reconciliation proved

COMG2026213751 — 687,704,212 against 687,704,212

The test policy now reconciles to Reinsurance’s own workbook to the cent, group by group — Property 346,134,212, Transportation 300,000,000, Motor 38,000,000, Guarantee 2,000,000, Miscellaneous 1,570,000.

Getting there meant rebuilding the read. We were taking sums insured from the coverage rows, which do not carry motor at all — 38,000,000 of this policy was invisible. The figures now come from the table the treaty actually cedes from.

Their own test cases then found two faults in our engine, and both are fixed. The facultative layer has no ceiling on a layered class — we had capped it at 50,000,000, generalising from motor evidence. And a nil sum insured is a data error to be raised, not a nil cession to be quietly written. Fourteen of their eighteen cases now reproduce exactly; the other four are the uncapped basis they instructed on 17 August and themselves reversed on 24 August, and our figures follow the later instruction.
